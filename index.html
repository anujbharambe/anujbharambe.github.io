Current research

Disentangling Capability, Reliability, and Mechanism in VLA Reinforcement Learning
In progress, 2026

Reinforcement learning improves vision-language-action models, but it is unclear whether it teaches robots new skills, unlocks skills they could already perform occasionally, or simply makes existing skills more reliable. We compare each RL-trained model with the imitation-trained model it started from, giving both many attempts from identical start states on standard tests of visual, semantic, and execution generalization, with controls that separate the effect of reward from extra training. We then identify which internal changes cause the improvement by transplanting them into the starting model or removing them, across multiple VLAs, benchmarks, and a real robot.

A Safety Benchmark for Robotic-Arm VLA Models
In progress, 2026

Building on our work on automated safety evaluation, we are developing a safety benchmark for vision-language-action models that control robotic arms, so different models can be tested and compared on how safely they act, not just whether they finish the task.

Real-Time Monitoring and Correction of LLM Agents
In progress, 2026

LLM agents working on long, multi-step tasks can gradually drift from what the user asked for while appearing to work correctly at every step. We are building a lightweight monitoring layer that detects this drift as it happens and corrects the agent before small errors compound.

Past Research

Physical Safety Evaluation of Robot Foundation Models
Anuj Bharambe, et al.
Under review at IEEE ICRA 2027  ·  US patent application filed

Robot foundation models, including vision-language-action (VLA) models, are usually judged by whether they complete a task, not by whether they do so safely. We develop an automated way to test the physical safety of these models and show that many task completions involve safety violations that success-only evaluation misses. Details will be shared after review.
